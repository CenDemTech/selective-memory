# Selective Memory: Chatbot Data Portability on Your Terms

A local mini app for filtering AI chat exports. Bring a `conversations.json`
file, review every conversation in a table, select the ones you care about, and
download a new JSON file with **the same structure** as the one you uploaded.

Claude and ChatGPT (OpenAI) exports are supported today. Gemini is not currently supported. 

> [!NOTE]
> Everything is private and runs locally. Nothing leaves your machine. Your upload is written to a temporary directory for
> the session, read by the local Flask process, and removed when you reset the
> session or the directory is cleared.

## Claude and ChatGPT Chat Supportability

The two exports are shaped very differently, so each has its own parser
([`claude_parser.py`](claude_parser.py), [`openai_parser.py`](openai_parser.py))
exposing the same interface; the app looks one up per session from `PARSERS` and
stays format-agnostic. Everything downstream — the table, sensitivity tagging,
the transcript highlighter, redaction/deletion, undo/redo, and the export/remove
build — works identically regardless of platform.

Both are a list of conversations at the top level. Below that they diverge:

| | **Claude** | **ChatGPT** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Messages** | A flat `chat_messages` list | A `mapping` — a *graph* of message nodes linked by `parent`/`children`, with `current_node` marking the active leaf |
| **Visible thread** | The list, in order | The path from `current_node` back to the root, so branches (edits, regenerations) are skipped |
| **Nodes dropped** | — | System, tool, and hidden nodes. The tool-authored answer node is kept |
| **Timestamps** | Already ISO | Unix `create_time` floats, converted to ISO so the UI formats them uniformly |
| **Edits key on** | `message_uuid` | `node_id`. Deleting re-links a node's children to its parent and moves `current_node` up if needed, so the exported graph stays valid |
| **`ID_FIELD`** | `uuid` | `id` |

`ID_FIELD` and `list_conversations` are all the app needs to filter and index
generically.

>[!WARNING]
> **Untitled conversations are never listed.** Conversations with a blank name do
> not appear in the table and cannot be selected, so an export leaves them out.
> Nothing in the interface mentions or counts them.

## Overview

The app has four pages, one per step. A chip row under each heading names them
all, ticking off completed steps in green and marking the current one active.

1. **Platform selection:** Choose whether your export came from **Anthropic**
   (Claude) or **Open AI** (ChatGPT). 
2. **Upload:** Drop your `conversations.json` onto the dropzone or browse for
   it to upload.
3. **Edit:** Every conversation is listed in a sortable, searchable table giving
   its name, start and update times, message and word counts, duration, and a
   sensitivity tag you can filter by tier. The sensitivity label determines whether the conversation contains potentially sensitive data. The label is generated by rule-based logic using RegEx run by a local script when your data is uploaded. No LLM or classifier calls are made. It scans the whole conversation, your messages and the assistant's replies.  Clicking **Review** button opens a transcript for
   redacting and deleting, and **Continue to export .json file** carries the
   selected rows forward.
4. **Export:** The selected conversations are shown read-only with their final
   sensitivity labels and two tally columns, **Redactions** and **Msgs deleted**,
   so you can see what each one lost before exporting. **Export .json
   conversation file** builds the download and saves it to your local downloads folder.


## Conversation sensitivity labels

Every conversation is tagged **Green**, **Yellow**, or **Red** by
[`sensitivity.py`](sensitivity.py). No model is involved: the tag comes from
pattern matching over 25 rules, run locally when your file is parsed. The
highest tier wins, so a single red signal makes the whole conversation red.

The tag covers the entire conversation, your messages and the model's replies
alike, because sensitive text matters wherever it lands. A conversation is Red
even if the credential appears only in the assistant's reply. Conversation
titles are the one exclusion, since they are generated rather than written and
cannot be highlighted in the transcript.

| Tag | Fires on |
| --- | --- |
| **Red** | User profile audits · regulated health/finance data · high-risk PII · live infrastructure secrets · malicious intent and exploits · legal and high-liability content |
| **Yellow** | Geographic and proximity queries · trackable indicators · memory retention requests · potential business ventures · interpersonal and professional communications · vague or indirect identifiers · corporate assets and IP · creative and copyrighted material |
| **Green** | Nothing matched |

Hovering a tag lists the categories that fired, and the **ⓘ** beside the
Sensitivity column header opens a panel explaining the scheme.

Opening **Review** on a Yellow or Red conversation highlights the exact runs
that triggered the tag, tinted by tier, with the category shown on hover. Both
tiers appear even in a Red conversation, because the yellow signals are still
worth seeing. A dropdown in the modal header counts the highlights and jumps
between them; Enter and Shift+Enter step through them and wrap at either end.
A Green conversation never shows a highlight, and its dropdown is hidden.

**What the tag will and will not catch.** The rules key on phrasing, so they
miss a sensitive topic described in words no rule anticipates. Structural checks
cut the obvious false positives: a card number must pass a Luhn checksum,
`192.168.1.50` reads as an IP address while `1.2.3.4` reads as a version number,
and `api_key = "your-api-key-here"` stays Green where a real-looking key goes
Red. Treat the tag as a way to find conversations worth reviewing, not as a
guarantee that Green ones are clean.

## Editing chat conversations

###  Redacting text

**Redact all** redacts every flagged run in the open conversation at once. To
redact a single run, click it and then click the **Redact ✕** button that
appears above it; clicking the run again deselects it.

A redacted run becomes a greyed-out box in place. The text stays visible so you
can see what you took out, but it is removed from the file you download. If the
same text appears more than once in a message, every copy is boxed.

Redaction is permanent until you undo it: the box survives closing and
reopening the conversation. It is also reversible with the modal's undo button.
Redacting changes only this session's working copy — the file you uploaded is
never touched, and re-uploading resets everything.

Because the sensitivity tag and word count are recalculated from the redacted
text, redacting every flagged run in a Red conversation turns it Green.

> [!TIP]
>  **Highlight mode** is the highlighter-pen toggle in the header, on by default
> whenever the conversation is editable. With it on, drag-select any text in a
> message and click the **Redact ✕** button that appears to redact exactly that
> run. This is how you remove text the rules never flagged. Turning it off makes
> text selection behave normally again.

## Deleting messages and turns

The transcript is grouped into turns, each a prompt and the replies that follow
it. Every turn and every message carries a checkbox and a trash icon.

There are three ways to remove content from a transcript:

- **Delete a message or a whole turn.** The trash icon on a message removes it;
  the trash icon on a turn removes every message that turn still holds. A
  deleted message leaves a tombstone reading *"This message has been deleted."*
  so the removal is visible, and undo brings it back.
- **Delete a batch.** Ticking message checkboxes builds a selection, and a turn
  checkbox ticks all of that turn's remaining messages at once. The footer
  button switches from **Delete Entire Conversation** to **Delete N messages**
  while anything is selected, so the two never compete for the same slot.
- **Redact any text.** Drag-select it with Highlight mode on and click **Redact
  ✕**, as described above.

**Delete Entire Conversation** removes the whole open conversation and closes
the modal. It asks you to confirm, because unlike the other actions it cannot
be undone.

All of these rewrite only the session's working copy, never your uploaded file,
and they recalculate the row — so the message count and sensitivity tag both
move. Deleting every message leaves the conversation present but empty.

A modified row shows a blue pill counting all of its edits, with a breakdown
beneath the conversation name such as "1 redaction · 1 message deleted". The
modal footer shows the same total as a revision count.

### Deleting whole conversations

Every row carries a trashcan, and the **Delete** button above the table removes
all selected conversations at once.

Deleting is reversible. A deleted conversation stays in place as a greyed-out
row with its name struck through, marked *Deleted*, and is simply left out of
the export. The undo and redo arrows beside the **Delete** button bring it back
or remove it again, which is why deleting never asks you to confirm. The footer
count carries a `· N deleted` tail while any deleted rows remain.

### Undo and redo

The **↶** and **↷** buttons in the modal footer, or Cmd/Ctrl+Z and
Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z, undo and redo edits to the open conversation — redactions and
deletions alike. Making a fresh edit after an undo discards the redo branch,
exactly as in a text editor. Undo back to the beginning and the row stops
counting as modified. History is kept per conversation for the session, and
re-uploading resets it.

## How the output stays structurally identical

The filtered file is rebuilt from the original parsed JSON objects, matched by
each platform's id field, rather than regenerated from the parsed table. Every
field on every conversation you keep survives untouched, including any the
platform adds later. The only difference between the file you uploaded and the
one you download is which conversations are in the list, and the removal of
anything you redacted or deleted.

## Implementation details

### Themes and branding

The app ships a light and a dark theme, light by default, with a toggle in the
top bar. Your choice is remembered between sessions. A single blue accent
carries every primary action in both themes, and the sensitivity colours stay a
green/amber/red traffic light.

The palette lives at the top of [`static/style.css`](static/style.css) as design
tokens. No component hardcodes a colour, so the dark theme is a token override
rather than a second stylesheet, and retheming means editing one block.

Roboto and Inter are bundled as local font files in
[`static/fonts/`](static/fonts) rather than loaded from a CDN, because fetching
a webfont on every page load would contradict the promise that nothing leaves
your machine. Their licences ship beside them.

The top bar carries the Selective Memory name and mark on the left, which
returns you to platform selection, and the theme toggle and CDT logo on the
right. A footer credit sits at the bottom of every page.
